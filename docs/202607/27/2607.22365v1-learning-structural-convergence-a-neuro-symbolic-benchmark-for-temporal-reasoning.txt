Abstract 

High-complexity operational environments require methods that detect and anticipate temporally distributed patterns rather than classify isolated events. This paper introduces TRACTA (Temporal Reasoning and Capability-Trajectory Analy-sis), a controlled synthetic benchmark for temporal structural reasoning in high-complexity event-driven systems, instantiated through Multi-Domain Operations (MDO)-like sce-narios. The benchmark includes three tasks: early_warning , pattern_detection , and 

run_classification , and compares raw-event neural models, a contract-lite semantic baseline, and a neuro-symbolic configuration operating on semantically grounded trajectories. Results show that raw event-level learning remains informative, but learned temporal modeling over semantic capability and contextual direct-impact trajectories achieves the highest aggregate point estimates, with the largest margins on the temporal tasks. Ablation analysis indicates that capability dynamics, contextual impacts, and temporal structure contribute complementary information. Shortcut diagnostics indicate that the most direct cross-run global-identifier shortcut is controlled in the primary neural input view, while residual shallow signals remain. Overall, the findings support a bounded methodological conclusion: in controlled syn-thetic settings, semantically grounded trajectories provide an effective representation for temporal structural reasoning, supporting further investigation of semantic interfaces be-tween event data, structured representations, and temporal learning. 

Keywords: temporal reasoning; structural convergence; neuro-symbolic learning; semantic trajectories; synthetic benchmark; early warning

Addressing this problem, therefore, requires reasoning over temporal accumulation, contextual impact, and interactions among capabilities. Raw event-level neural models provide a natural baseline, as they can learn temporal dependencies directly from sequential data. At the same time, such models may be sensitive to superficial regularities, especially in controlled or synthetic datasets. # 2 Related Work 

2.1 Neuro-Symbolic AI and Knowledge-Guided Learning 

Neuro-symbolic artificial intelligence provides a broad family of approaches that combine neural learning with symbolic representations, logical constraints, knowledge bases, or structured reasoning mechanisms. Recent surveys emphasize that this family is heterogeneous: symbolic knowledge may be used to structure inputs, constrain learning, support reasoning, regularize model behavior, or improve interpretability, rather than define a single unified architecture [ 1 –3 ]. This distinction is important for TRACTA because the neuro_symbolic configuration studied in this paper does not replace learning with symbolic inference. Instead, it uses an ontology-mediated reference layer to transform event observations into semantic capability and contextual direct-impact trajectories, over which temporal learning is then performed. Several neuro-symbolic models illustrate different ways of linking learning and reasoning. DeepProbLog integrates neural predicates with probabilistic logic programming, thereby con-necting subsymbolic perception with probabilistic logical inference [ 4]. Logic Tensor Networks provide a differentiable framework in which logical formulas can be represented through fuzzy semantics and optimized jointly with neural components [ 5]. These approaches differ from the TRACTA setting, where the symbolic component is not optimized jointly with the neural model and the semantic_baseline is not treated as a full symbolic oracle. Nevertheless, they motivate the broader methodological question of how explicit structured knowledge can be combined with learning systems. A related line of work concerns knowledge-guided or theory-guided machine learning. Reviews on domain-knowledge integration highlight that prior knowledge can enter neural models through features, constraints, architectures, loss functions, or intermediate represen-tations [ 6]. Theory-guided data science similarly argues that scientific or domain knowledge can help structure learning problems in settings where purely data-driven models may be insufficiently interpretable or poorly aligned with known system relationships [ 7]. More recent surveys on integrating scientific knowledge with machine learning make a comparable point for engineering and environmental systems, where learned models often need to interact with explicit system structure and prior knowledge [ 8 ]. TRACTA follows this general orientation, but with a bounded scope: it evaluates whether semantic trajectories derived from a controlled operational reference layer provide a useful substrate for early_warning and pattern_detection in synthetic high-complexity scenarios. 

2.2 Temporal Pattern Detection and Early Warning 

The temporal dimension of TRACTA connects the benchmark to time-series classification and sequence learning. Deep learning has become a major approach for time-series classification, with convolutional, recurrent, and hybrid architectures used to learn discriminative temporal representations from sequential data [ 9]. Architectures such as InceptionTime further show the importance of strong temporal baselines when evaluating new representations or model-ing choices for time-series classification [ 10 ]. In TRACTA, raw event-level neural baselines and transformer_raw_event are therefore necessary comparators: the benchmark does not assume that raw event sequences are uninformative, but asks whether they remain the strongest configuration once the main global-identifier shortcut is controlled. 3The early_warning task is also related to early classification of time series, where the objective is to make reliable predictions before the full sequence has been observed. Early work formalized the problem as classifying time series from partial observations, while later work emphasized the trade-off between earliness and accuracy in time-sensitive applications [ 11 , 12 ]. This literature is relevant to TRACTA because early_warning evaluates whether a model can anticipate future activation of structural labels from a finite observation window, rather than classify a complete trajectory after the fact. The benchmark differs from standard early time-series classification, however, because its labels are defined over capability-state degradation, structural event-context filters, and persistence conditions rather than over conventional class labels attached to complete time series. Early warning has also been studied in application domains where heterogeneous sensor or event streams must be interpreted before the full phenomenon unfolds. For example, machine-learning approaches to earthquake early warning use distributed multi-sensor data to detect and characterize hazardous events before their effects reach a location [ 13 ]. TRACTA is not an earthquake-warning benchmark, but it shares the methodological concern of prediction under partial temporal evidence. At the architectural level, attention-based sequence models provide another relevant comparator. The Transformer introduced self-attention as an alternative to purely recurrent sequence modeling [ 14 ]. Transformer-based representation learning has also been applied to multivariate time series, motivating the inclusion of transformer_raw_event as a stronger raw-event temporal baseline [ 15 ]. The purpose of this baseline is not to exhaust the space of possible attention-based event models, but to reduce the risk that the neuro_symbolic configuration is compared only against weak raw-event learners. 

2.3 Synthetic Benchmarks, Shortcut Learning, and Robust Evaluation 

Synthetic data and simulation-based benchmarks are useful when the target phenomenon is difficult to observe, annotate, or control in real data. Synthetic-data surveys emphasize that generated data can support controlled evaluation and model development, while also requiring caution because synthetic distributions may encode assumptions or artifacts of the generator [ 16 ]. More broadly, work on dataset development in machine learning highlights that datasets are not neutral containers of examples: their construction choices, documentation, and benchmark use affect what model performance can validly be interpreted to mean [ 17 ]. Related work on data statements similarly argues that explicit dataset documentation can help clarify provenance, intended use, and limits of generalization [ 18 ]. These concerns are central to TRACTA, which is designed as a controlled methodological benchmark rather than as an operational validation. A major threat in benchmark design is that models may exploit dataset-specific shortcuts rather than the intended construct. Dataset bias has long been recognized as a challenge for cross-dataset generalization and benchmark validity [ 19 ]. Shortcut learning research further shows that deep neural networks may rely on simple, predictive cues that perform well on benchmark distributions but do not correspond to the desired underlying reasoning process [ 20 ]. In machine-learning-based science, leakage can produce inflated or non-reproducible performance estimates when information from the evaluation target is inadvertently made available during training or model selection [21]. TRACTA addresses one specific shortcut by separating the full raw audit view from the primary run-local aliased neural view. This design controls the globally stable target and location identifier shortcut in the primary raw neural input, while leaving residual shallow signals as explicit validity concerns rather than claiming that all shortcuts have been eliminated. Robust evaluation also requires uncertainty-aware and reproducible reporting. Bootstrap confidence intervals provide a classical tool for estimating uncertainty around performance statistics without reducing evaluation to a single point estimate [ 22 ]. In machine learning, uncertainty estimates and model confidence can themselves be sensitive to dataset shift, which motivates cautious interpretation of reported performance in non-identically distributed set-4tings [ 23 ]. Benchmark comparisons can also be affected by variance due to data sampling, initialization, hyperparameters, and other experimental choices [ 24 ]. Reproducibility initia-tives in machine learning have therefore emphasized the importance of sharing code, data, configuration details, and experimental artifacts where possible [ 25 ]. TRACTA incorporates these concerns through run-level split policies, shortcut diagnostics, ablation configurations, bootstrap uncertainty estimates, and reproducibility assets. These elements support a bounded methodological interpretation of the results, not a claim of operational readiness. 

2.4 High-Complexity Operational Environments and MDO-like Reasoning 

The operational motivation for TRACTA comes from decision-support settings in which hetero-geneous events interact over time and produce system-level effects. Situation awareness research provides a useful conceptual background because it frames decision support in dynamic systems as involving perception, comprehension, and projection of evolving states [ 26 ]. Research on collective behavior and complex systems similarly emphasizes that distributed interactions can generate emergent dynamics that are not reducible to isolated events or actors [ 27 ]. In the defense context, NATO doctrine describes contemporary operations as joint and multi-domain, involving coordinated activity across physical and non-physical domains [28]. This literature motivates the use of MDO-like scenarios as a demanding instance of a broader methodological problem: detecting and anticipating structural convergence in high-complexity operational trajectories. However, TRACTA should not be read as a doctrinal model of MDO or as an operationally validated decision-support system. Its contribution is narrower. It provides a controlled synthetic benchmark in which raw event-level learning, a contract-lite semantic comparator, and ontology-mediated temporal learning can be evaluated under common tasks, shortcut controls, ablations, uncertainty estimates, and reproducibility conventions. The related work therefore situates TRACTA at the intersection of neuro-symbolic AI, temporal learning, benchmark validity, and high-complexity operational reasoning, while keeping the empirical claim bounded to the synthetic setting studied in this paper. 

# 3 Problem Framing and Benchmark Rationale 

This section defines the decision-support problem addressed by TRACTA and motivates the benchmark design. The central construct is structural convergence : the emergence of temporally and contextually meaningful degradation patterns over capability trajectories, rather than separability over isolated event tokens or superficial event statistics. 

3.1 High-Complexity Operational Trajectories 

TRACTA is motivated by settings in which relevant signals are distributed across time, targets, lo-cations, actors, and capability dependencies. This framing is consistent with situation-awareness research and with the treatment of Multi-Domain Operations (MDO) as a joint, multi-domain operating context [ 26 , 28 ]. In such environments, cyber disruptions, electromagnetic interference, logistics delays, kinetic actions, information effects, infrastructure degradation, or changes in coalition support may be meaningful locally but insufficient in isolation. The analytical problem is whether heterogeneous observations jointly indicate a broader pattern of degradation. A localized event may become significant only when combined with other events that affect related capabilities, occur within reinforcing temporal windows, or propagate through shared dependencies. TRACTA therefore treats MDO-like trajectories as a controlled synthetic instance of a broader problem: detecting and anticipating structural convergence in temporally evolving operational systems. 53.2 Structural Convergence as the Target Construct 

A trajectory is structurally convergent when heterogeneous events, distributed across time and operational contexts, jointly produce capability-level degradation patterns that satisfy the benchmark label conditions. The target is therefore not the occurrence of a specific event type, subtype, or entity token, but the emergence of a meaningful pattern over the operational state. Raw event-level learning is a necessary comparator because event logs contain useful tempo-ral and contextual information, including timestamps, event types, actors, targets, locations, confidence, and severity. Neural sequence models may capture regularities that are difficult to specify manually. However, in synthetic benchmarks, stable identifiers, recurring locations, fixed temporal templates, or repeated entity combinations may become unintended proxy labels. This creates a risk that models exploit shortcuts or leakage paths rather than the intended construct [20, 21]. Semantic and rule-based approaches address a complementary aspect of the problem. They encode relations among events, targets, locations, and capabilities, and make explicit how local impacts propagate into capability trajectories. This supports interpretability and aligns with neuro-symbolic and knowledge-guided learning perspectives [ 1 , 8]. However, fixed thresholds and deterministic rules may be sensitive to calibration and may underutilize noisy, delayed, or weakly distributed temporal evidence. For this reason, the semantic configuration in TRACTA is treated as a contract-lite comparator rather than a full symbolic oracle. The neuro-symbolic formulation combines these two perspectives. The semantic component maps raw events into capability and contextual direct-impact trajectories using structured knowledge about event subtypes, targets, locations, capability dependencies, and propagation effects. The learning component then models temporal dependencies over these trajectories. The hypothesis is bounded: within a controlled synthetic benchmark, semantic contextualization combined with temporal learning may provide a stronger basis for early warning and pattern detection than raw-event learning or contract-lite semantic rules alone.

3.5 Shortcut Leakage and Benchmark Design 

Synthetic benchmarks enable control over scenario generation, semantics, labels, and evaluation, but they may also introduce artificial correlations. In TRACTA, the main shortcut risk concerns globally stable target and location identifiers. If these identifiers recur across runs in label-correlated ways, raw neural models may learn identifier–label associations rather than capability-level effects. Let gT (e) and gL(e) denote the global target and location identifiers of event e. The full raw audit view preserves these identifiers for traceability, semantic resolution, and leakage auditing. The primary raw neural input view instead applies a run-local aliasing map

Thus, the same global target or location does not retain a stable identity across different runs, while within-run recurrence is preserved. The anti-leakage claim is limited. TRACTA controls the most direct cross-run identifier shortcut in the primary neural input view, but residual shallow signals may remain, including within-run recurrence, temporal organization, confidence, and severity patterns, and other benchmark-specific cues. These signals are treated as validity concerns rather than as evidence of the complete elimination of leakage. Accordingly, TRACTA compares raw neural, semantic, and neuro-symbolic configurations on the same controlled synthetic trajectories. The benchmark defines labels over structural convergence, includes raw-event baselines, includes a contract-lite semantic comparator, and tests whether ontology-mediated temporal learning over capability and contextual direct-impact trajectories provides the strongest aggregate configuration among the tested alterna-tives. The claim remains bounded, with primary evidence expected from early_warning and 

pattern_detection .

# 4 Methodology: Semantic Reference Layer and Benchmark Construc-tion 

This section describes how TRACTA is generated, represented, labeled, split, and audited. Consistent with the problem framing in Section 3, the benchmark operationalizes structural convergence through synthetic runs, semantic trajectory construction, contract-based pattern labeling, controlled dataset views, run-level splitting, and shortcut-control mechanisms. Model architectures, training procedures, ablations, metrics, and uncertainty estimates are introduced separately in Section 5. 84.1 Synthetic MDO-Like Runs 

TRACTA uses synthetic MDO-like runs because the target phenomenon is difficult to evaluate under controlled conditions using operational data alone. Real event streams may be incomplete, sensitive, non-repeatable, affected by confounding factors, or lack reliable ground truth for latent convergence patterns. A synthetic benchmark allows scenario generation, label definition, dataset views, semantic resolution, and split policy to be explicitly controlled [16, 17]. The purpose is not to reproduce a real operation in full fidelity but to provide a controlled methodological testbed for representation and model comparison. In TRACTA, heterogeneous events distributed across time, targets, and locations are generated to evaluate their effects on capability trajectories. The benchmark, therefore, supports controlled comparisons among raw neural, semantic, and neuro-symbolic configurations under the same scenario logic without claiming operational readiness.

4.6 Dataset Construction and Split Policy 

TRACTA contains 200 synthetic runs, divided into 100 positive and 100 negative runs. Each run has a horizon of H = 96. Temporal-window datasets use a window size of w = 8 and forecasting leads of ℓ ∈ { 3, 6, 12 } for early_warning . The resulting early-warning corpus contains 49,200 temporal windows. Table 1 summarizes the main dataset parameters. The train–validation–test split is performed by run_id , not by temporal sample. This is es-sential because early_warning and pattern_detection samples are derived from overlapping windows within the same run. A temporal-window split would allow highly dependent samples from the same trajectory to appear in different partitions, creating evaluation leakage [ 21 ]. The resulting partition contains 140 training runs, 30 validation runs, and 30 test runs. The main experiments use a family-balanced run-level split, as described in Section 5. 

4.7 Dataset Views and Shortcut Control 

The benchmark builder exports multiple views of the same underlying runs, summarized in Table 2. 5.1 Benchmark Scope and Split Policy 

All experiments are conducted on the TRACTA benchmark described in Section 4.6. The benchmark contains 200 synthetic runs with horizon H = 96. Temporal-window tasks use window size w = 8, and early_warning uses forecasting leads ℓ ∈ { 3, 6, 12 }.All reported experiments use a run-level train–validation–test split. The split is performed by run_id , not by temporal window, to prevent overlapping windows from the same trajectory from appearing in different partitions. The main experiments use a family-balanced run-level split, balancing by family_id and run_label . The resulting partition contains 140 training runs, 30 validation runs, and 30 test runs, with class balance 70/70 in training, 15/15 in validation, and 15/15 in test.

# 7 Discussion 

The results support the comparative thesis in a bounded sense. Within TRACTA, the 

neuro_symbolic configuration provides the strongest aggregate performance, particularly on the temporal-window tasks of early_warning and pattern_detection . This does not imply general superiority but indicates that, under controlled experimental conditions, 18 semantically grounded trajectories combined with temporal learning constitute the most effective configuration among those tested. 

7.1 Representation-Level Interpretation 

The observed advantage is best interpreted at the level of representation. In contrast to raw-event baselines, the neuro-symbolic configuration operates on semantically grounded trajectories, encoding contextual direct impacts and accumulated capability states over time. This representation is aligned with the target construct of structural convergence, defined in terms of capability degradation, persistence, and contextual constraints. Temporal learning therefore operates directly over structured state variables, rather than inferring such structure from event tokens. The results are consistent with this formulation: when the task is defined in terms of capability-level structural convergence, semantically grounded trajectories provide a more suitable substrate for temporal modeling than raw event sequences. This interpretation remains bound to the tested benchmark setting. 

7.2 Temporal Tasks and Structural Convergence 

The clearest evidence comes from the temporal window tasks. Both early_warning and 

pattern_detection directly evaluate reasoning over evolving structural convergence. For early_warning , the improvement indicates that semantically grounded trajectories support anticipation from partial observations. For pattern_detection , the larger gap is consis-tent with the label definition: detecting active patterns requires reasoning over accumulated capability states and contextual structure. By contrast, run_classification operates on complete trajectories and can rely more heavily on aggregate signals. This explains the reduced performance gap and supports its role as a complementary endpoint rather than as the primary test of structural convergence. The aggregate pattern is not uniform across all settings: a small number of early-warning target–lead combinations are led by comparator models, and the grouped-bootstrap intervals overlap, particularly for run_classification . These qualifications are detailed in Appendices C and E. 

7.3 Role of Raw Event-Level Learning 

Raw event-level learning remains informative across all tasks, confirming that the event sur-face retains a predictive signal even after shortcut control. This is particularly evident in 

run_classification , where raw baselines remain competitive. The inclusion of transformer_raw_event shows that this result is not due to weak baselines. Increasing model capacity does not change the overall ranking, suggesting that the main difference arises from representation rather than architecture. More expressive raw-event architectures do not, however, uniformly outperform simpler raw baselines. The strongest raw-event configuration varies across tasks: advanced_neural obtains the highest raw-event score on early_warning and pattern_detection , whereas simple_neural is the strongest raw-event comparator on run_classification . Increased architectural complexity alone therefore does not guarantee consistent performance gains across the tested tasks. At the same time, raw-event models do not fully match the performance obtained with semantically grounded trajectories on the temporal tasks, indicating that event-level information alone does not provide the most effective representation for structural convergence. 19 7.4 Semantic and Temporal Contributions 

The ablation results indicate that the neuro-symbolic advantage is compositional. Both capabil-ity trajectories and contextual direct-impact trajectories contribute to performance, and their combination yields the strongest results. The shuffled-semantic ablation refines this interpretation. Its competitiveness on 

early_warning shows that aggregate semantic information carries a predictive signal. Its degradation on pattern_detection shows that temporal organization is critical when detecting active structural convergence. Overall, the results indicate that performance arises from the interaction between semanti-cally grounded trajectories and temporal learning rather than from either component in isolation. 

7.5 Limits of Rule-Only Semantic Reasoning 

The semantic_baseline confirms that explicit semantic structure is informative but not suffi-cient. While it captures part of the signal through rules over semantically grounded trajectories, it underperforms compared to the neuro-symbolic configuration, which can exploit temporal variation and weak or distributed evidence. This result is specific to the contract-lite comparator used here. It should not be interpreted as a general limitation of symbolic reasoning, but as evidence that fixed rules alone do not fully capture temporal structural convergence in this benchmark setting. Stronger symbolic, probabilistic, temporal-logic, or graph-based reasoning systems may yield different comparative results and are not evaluated in the present benchmark. 

7.6 Shortcut Control and Validity Boundaries 

The shortcut diagnostics support the benchmark design. The high predictivity of the full raw audit view confirms the presence of a strong global-identifier shortcut. The reduced performance of the run-local aliased view indicates that the most direct cross-run global-identifier shortcut is controlled in the primary neural input. However, residual shallow signals remain, particularly through within-run alias recurrence. Such recurrence may reflect both benchmark-specific bias and genuinely meaningful repeated effects on the same targets or locations; it should therefore be treated as an interpretive boundary rather than classified uniformly as leakage. This is consistent with the bounded anti-leakage claim: the benchmark removes the most direct cross-run shortcut but does not eliminate all possible signals. These residual cues define validity boundaries rather than flaws. They reflect both potential bias and genuinely informative event structure and should therefore be interpreted with caution. 

7.7 Implications for Neuro-Symbolic Design 

The results suggest a structured role for the semantic reference layer as an interface between event data and temporal learning. In TRACTA, events are mapped into semantically grounded trajectories, making capability-level effects explicit and auditable. More specifically, the semantic layer contextualizes heterogeneous events, represents their effects as explicit capability-level states, and provides the temporal learner with a structured trajectory space over which accumu-lation, persistence, and interaction effects can be modeled. This supports a division of labor in which structured knowledge organizes the representation space, while learning captures dependencies that unfold across time. The implication remains methodological: the benchmark does not establish operational readiness but indicates that ontology-mediated trajectories can support more effective integration of event data and temporal modeling in controlled settings. 20 7.8 Summary 

The discussion supports a bounded and coherent interpretation aligned with the problem framing. Semantically grounded trajectories improve performance on tasks requiring temporal structural reasoning; raw event-level learning remains informative but not dominant; and the strongest results arise from combining semantic contextualization with temporal modeling. The advantage is most evident in temporal-window tasks, while residual shortcut signals and the benchmark’s synthetic nature define explicit validity boundaries. This interpretation should remain bounded. The results do not show that the specific neuro-symbolic architecture is uni-versally superior, nor that raw event-level learning cannot detect complex patterns. They show that, under the tested task definitions, dataset views, split policy, and model configurations, the combination of semantic contextualization and learned temporal modeling yields the strongest aggregate result. 

# 8 Limitations and Threats to Validity 

This section summarizes the main limitations that bound the interpretation of the results. The reported findings should be understood as evidence within a controlled synthetic benchmark, not as validation in operational environments. 

8.1 Synthetic Setting and External Validity 

The primary limitation is TRACTA’s synthetic nature. While synthetic benchmarks enable controlled evaluation of structural convergence under explicit assumptions, they do not establish real-world validity [ 16 , 17 ]. Real operational environments involve incomplete and noisy observations, uncertain event attribution, evolving conditions, and human decision processes that are not modeled here. At the same time, synthetic construction provides a practical testbed for problems where real-world data are unavailable, sensitive, or difficult to label. The results should therefore be interpreted as methodological evidence under controlled assumptions. External validity is also conditioned by the specific domain instantiation. TRACTA adopts MDO-like structures, including capability dependencies, event schema, and pattern definitions. While the underlying approach—mapping events into semantically grounded trajectories and detecting structural convergence—is not domain-specific, its generalization to other settings has yet to be empirically demonstrated. 

8.2 Benchmark Size and Statistical Uncertainty 

The benchmark includes 200 independent runs. Although temporal-window tasks generate many samples, these samples are not independent observations because they are derived from overlapping segments of the same trajectories. The run-level split mitigates this issue, but the number of independent runs remains limited. Results may therefore be sensitive to alternative split realizations, scenario distributions, or larger benchmark instances. Bootstrap confidence intervals characterize uncertainty over fixed saved prediction sets under grouped resampling by run_id , but do not capture uncertainty arising from alternative splits, retraining, or different scenario-generation processes. They should therefore be inter-preted as descriptive uncertainty context rather than as definitive statistical guarantees. Further details are reported in Appendix E. 21 8.3 Semantic Representation and Construct Alignment 

A central construct validity limitation concerns the proximity between the semantic reference layer and the label-generation procedure. Both are defined over capability trajectories and structural conditions. This alignment is part of the representation hypothesis, but it favors configurations operating on semantically grounded trajectories. The results, therefore, do not establish that the same advantage would hold under noisy, incomplete, or misspecified semantic layers, as would be expected in real-world settings. The ablation analysis mitigates this concern by showing that performance is not reducible to a single component, but does not eliminate it. In addition, task interpretation remains important. The primary conclusions are supported by early_warning and pattern_detection , which directly evaluate temporal structural con-vergence. Performance on run_classification is less diagnostic and should not be overinter-preted. More generally, aggregate performance should not be interpreted as uniform dominance across individual targets or forecasting leads. The observed setting-level heterogeneity is re-ported in Appendices C and E. 

8.4 Shortcut Control and Residual Signals 

The benchmark controls the most direct shortcut by removing globally stable target and location identifiers from the primary raw-event input view. However, it does not eliminate all shallow signals. In particular, run-local aliasing preserves within-run recurrence, which remains informative. Such recurrence may reflect both genuine operational structure and residual bias. TRACTA therefore controls the most direct cross-run global-identifier shortcut in the primary neural input view, while residual shallow signals remain and are treated as validity boundaries. The corresponding diagnostics are reported in Appendix D. 

8.5 Model Scope and Reproducibility Boundaries 

The conclusions are limited to the tested model space, including raw-event baselines, a Trans-former baseline, a contract-lite semantic comparator, and a recurrent neuro-symbolic configura-tion. Alternative architectures—such as larger attention-based models, graph-based methods, or stronger symbolic systems—may yield different results. Reproducibility is supported through disclosed configurations, splits, and outputs, but exact replication may depend on implementation details such as the software environment, preprocessing, and random seeds. Finally, even synthetic scenarios may encode domain-specific structures. Distribution of the benchmark artifacts is therefore subject to applicable institutional, sensitivity, and distribution restrictions. 

8.6 Summary 

Overall, the limitations define a clear boundary of validity. The results provide controlled evidence that semantically grounded trajectories improve performance on temporal structural convergence tasks within TRACTA. Extending this conclusion to real-world settings will require validation under realistic data conditions, alternative semantic constructions, and broader model spaces. 22 9 Conclusions and Future Work 

This paper introduced TRACTA, a controlled benchmark for evaluating structural convergence over temporally evolving capability trajectories. The results support a bounded comparative conclusion: semantically grounded trajectories, combined with temporal learning, achieve the strongest aggregate point estimates among the tested configurations, with the clearest advantage on the early-warning and pattern-detection tasks. Raw event-level learning remains informative but does not represent the strongest overall configuration under the benchmark conditions. This aggregate result should nevertheless be interpreted in light of overlapping uncertainty intervals and localized setting-level heterogeneity. The contribution is methodological. TRACTA highlights the role of semantic reference layers in mapping event data into structured trajectories for temporal modeling. Future work will focus on three directions: (i) robustness across larger and more diverse benchmark instances, (ii) sensitivity to imperfect or noisy semantic representations, and (iii) evaluation across broader model classes and more realistic data conditions. Overall, the benchmark provides a controlled basis for studying representation choices in temporal structural reasoning.

A.8 Implementation Boundaries 

The TRACTA v1.0.0 release uses the contract-based structural path described above. Temporal labels are generated from the complete synthetic trajectory and include the future recovery gate. They should therefore be interpreted as offline structural annotations, not as labels computed strictly from event prefixes. Contextual direct-impact trajectories and accumulated capability trajectories play distinct roles. Direct-impact trajectories preserve resolved local event consequences, while capability trajectories represent accumulated capability state after propagation, persistence, decay, and clipping. The neuro-symbolic configuration uses both trajectory families as inputs, whereas pattern labels are defined through capability-state conditions and structural filters. The appendix specifies the label construction used for the reported benchmark release. # D Ablation and Shortcut Diagnostics 

This appendix complements the aggregate ablation results reported in Table 8. Table D.1 presents the signed macro-F1 differences relative to the full neuro-symbolic configuration. The isolated capability and direct-impact representations remain informative but do not match the performance of the full configuration. Temporal shuffling produces the largest degradation on pattern detection, supporting the role of temporal organization in identifying active structural patterns. As shown in Table D.2, exposing global identifiers creates a strong shortcut. Run-local aliasing substantially reduces this direct cross-run signal, while the recurrence diagnostics indicate that residual within-run structural regularities remain informative. TRACTA therefore controls the most direct global-identifier shortcut across runs in the primary neural input view. Residual shallow signals nevertheless remain and are explicitly documented as a validity boundary of the benchmark. 

# E Bootstrap and Setting-Level Analyses 

Bootstrap intervals were computed from fixed saved prediction sets using 1,000 grouped resam-ples, with run_id as the resampling unit. The headline values reported in Table E.1 summarize the setting-level point estimates and corresponding bootstrap bounds. They should therefore be interpreted as descriptive means of setting-level summaries rather than as confidence intervals obtained from a joint bootstrap of each task-level aggregate. The neuro-symbolic configuration achieves the highest headline point estimate on all three tasks. Its setting-level interval summaries nevertheless overlap with those of the comparator models. These intervals are therefore reported as uncertainty context and should not be inter-preted as definitive evidence of statistical significance at the task-aggregate level. Uncertainty is particularly broad for run classification. Among the ablations, the clearest degradation is observed for shuffled semantic trajectories on pattern detection.
