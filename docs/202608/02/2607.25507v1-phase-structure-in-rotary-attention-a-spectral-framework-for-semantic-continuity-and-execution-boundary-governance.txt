Abstract 

Transformer language models are usually analyzed through vector geometry, yet ordered context and rotary position encoding introduce explicit phase structure into query–key interac-tions. This paper develops a bounded spectral framework for examining rotary phase alignment, hidden-state continuity, and semantic drift without treating language models as literal physical wave systems. It first identifies ordered hidden-state sequences, rather than vocabulary indices, as valid domains for spectral decomposition. It then derives the Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE) attention score as a sum of magnitude-weighted cosine terms and proves a local stability lemma: uniformly bounded phase displacement limits degradation of the corresponding pre-softmax score. To extend phase analysis beyond native RoPE coordinates, the paper defines complex modal coordinates over fixed orthonormal direction pairs and introduces a weighted coherence functional for hidden-state trajectories. These constructions support a strict distinction between representational continuity and execution-boundary admissibility. Internal coherence may de-scribe preservation of task-relevant relations, but it cannot authorize a consequential transition. Positioned against existing geometric, spectral, phase-modulation, representation-analysis, and mechanistic-interpretability accounts, the framework contributes a theoretical and methodological program for determining when spectral structure explains continuity and when governance must remain an external predicate over execution. 

Keywords: transformers; rotary position embedding; RoPE; phase geometry; spectral analysis; semantic continuity; representation drift; AI governance; execution boundaries 

# 1 Introduction 

Modern language models are ordinarily described through geometry. Tokens are embedded as vectors, transformed through successive layers, compared by inner products, and converted into probability distributions over possible continuations. That account is correct and indispensable. It identifies the spaces in which representations live and the operators through which those representations are transformed. Geometry, however, does not exhaust the available mathematical description. Transformer com-putation also contains ordered trajectories, periodic positional operators, phase-sensitive correlations, and distributed modes whose alignment changes across context. Rotary Position Embedding makes this especially explicit: position is implemented as coordinated rotation, and relative displacement appears directly inside the query–key interaction.[ 2] Once that structure is recognized, parts of transformer inference admit a spectral treatment in which contextual compatibility can be examined 1arXiv:2607.25507v1 [cs.CL] 28 Jul 2026 through phase alignment and semantic continuity through the preservation of task-relevant modal relations. 

1.1 Related work and contribution boundary 

The framework lies at the intersection of Transformer attention, rotary position encoding, rep-resentation analysis, and mechanistic interpretability. Transformer literature defines attention as content-dependent query–key compatibility followed by normalized allocation.[ 1 ] RoPE makes relative position algebraically available through blockwise rotations of queries and keys.[ 2] Centered kernel alignment and related representation-analysis methods compare learned spaces while warning against treating arbitrary coordinates as intrinsically meaningful.[ 4] Mechanistic interpretability seeks causal or circuit-level accounts of computations implemented by attention heads, including induction-head behavior.[5] Three closer lines of work establish the necessary novelty boundary. Barbero et al. analyze how trained models use RoPE frequencies, associating high-frequency components with robust positional patterns, finding preferential use of low frequencies, and hypothesizing that those frequencies carry semantic information.[ 6 ] Gu et al. develop a spectral analysis of content-position coupling through Toeplitz structures derived from attention logits and study the resulting contraction properties.[ 7 ]Liu formulates RoPE directly as phase modulation over complex oscillator banks and derives bounds involving context length, base frequency, depth, and numerical precision.[8] This paper therefore does not claim the first phase interpretation of RoPE, the first spectral analysis of positional encoding, a new encoding scheme, or an empirically discovered circuit. Its distinct contribution is to connect four elements not combined in those accounts: an exact decomposition of rotary attention into magnitude-weighted phase terms; a local bound on pre-softmax score loss under bounded phase displacement; a basis-disciplined construction for phase-sensitive continuity across generated-state trajectories; and a formal separation between representational continuity and execution-boundary admissibility. The last distinction is categorical: phase structure may diagnose preservation within a trajectory, but it cannot confer authority on the trajectory’s consequence. This paper develops that treatment without claiming that language models are physical wave systems. The terms spectral, phase, mode, and coherence refer to mathematical representations of ordered computational states. They do not imply propagating energy, quantum behavior, or literal oscillation in physical space. Rotary attention contains an explicit phase geometry, and that geometry provides a principled foundation for analyzing contextual reinforcement, long-range drift, and the separation between semantic coherence and institutional admissibility.

# 7 Autoregressive Inference as an Execution Trajectory 

A transformer should not be described as a conventional recurrent network. Autoregressive inference nevertheless produces a sequence of computational states. Let x≤t = ( x0, x 1, . . . , x t) be the generated prefix at step t. Let Et denote the complete execution state required to continue inference. Depending on implementation, it may include the token prefix, cached keys and values, decoding parameters, active system instructions, and external context supplied to the model. The next-token distribution is

ht = O(Et). (35) 7The sequence h0, h 1, . . . , h T then forms an analyzable trajectory through representation space. It is driven and nonlinear, affected by attention, residual pathways, normalization, nonlinear activation, token selection, context growth, and any external retrieval or tool results entering the execution state. A spectral account does not turn this process into a conservative oscillator. It provides coordinates for studying how selected relational structures change as execution proceeds. 

# 8 Semantic Continuity and Modal Drift 

A generated continuation can remain locally fluent while progressively departing from its initiating objective. This occurs because next-token plausibility is evaluated conditionally at each step. Local compatibility does not guarantee preservation of a global invariant. Let h0 represent an anchor state associated with the initial directive, established facts, or governing objective. Let ht be the selected observable at a later execution step. A geometric continuity measure in a task-relevant subspace P is 

κt = ⟨P h t, P h 0⟩∥P h t∥ ∥ P h 0∥ . (36) Using the paired modal construction, a phase-sensitive alternative is 

Ht = Γ( t, 0) . (37) The two quantities answer different questions. κt measures aggregate directional similarity after projection, while Ht resolves that relation through amplitude-weighted phase alignment across chosen modal pairs. The modal view supports a specific hypothesis: A trajectory may preserve high local transition compatibility while accumulating global modal divergence from its anchor. This offers a disciplined interpretation of semantic drift. Topic drift may appear as progressive phase displacement in modes associated with the initiating subject. Contradiction may appear as unstable alternation or competition between modal configurations associated with incompatible commitments. Repetition may appear as excessive concentration around a narrow and persistent modal pattern. Unsupported factual elaboration may preserve linguistic and thematic coherence while diverging from modes associated with evidence-grounded constraints. These are empirical hypotheses. Their validity depends on whether the chosen basis reliably separates these failure classes from ordinary stylistic and semantic variation. The scientific value of the framework therefore rests on comparative prediction, not descriptive elegance.

# 10 Boundary Stabilization Without Internal Suppression 

The distinction between coherence and admissibility prevents a common architectural error. Gover-nance does not require eliminating variation from the model’s internal process. It requires controlling the transition at which variation becomes accepted consequence. 9Let T (st) be the set of candidate transitions the model can generate from state st, and let 

A(st) ⊆ T (st) (44) be the subset permitted by the governance contract. The model may explore broadly within T (st), while execution is restricted to A(st). The governed system therefore separates generative diversity from institutional authority: candidate diversity within T (st),

executed consequence within A(st). (45) This architecture does not demand that the model’s latent process be deterministic. It demands that the crossing into accepted action be governed. The phase analogy is useful only at this boundary level. An external governance system may compare an evolving interaction against a stable reference and intervene when the trajectory departs from authorized conditions. It does not literally phase-lock the model’s internal coordinates. Spectral analysis may describe continuity within the model’s trajectory; governance determines whether that trajectory may acquire institutional consequence.

Task-relevant paired modal coordinates can be learned or selected from hidden-state trajecto-ries. Their coherence measure Ht = Γ( t, 0) can then be tested as a predictor of objective drift, contradiction, repetition, or unsupported elaboration. 

11.3 Comparison with geometric baselines 

Any proposed phase-coherence measure must be tested against established alternatives, including cosine similarity, subspace distance, centered kernel alignment, linear-probe performance, and activation-space change metrics. Spectral analysis earns explanatory standing only if it improves prediction, separates failure classes, or reveals structure unavailable to simpler baselines. 

11.4 Positional-encoding comparison 

Models using RoPE should exhibit explicit relative-phase structure in query–key interactions. Comparable models using additive positional encoding should not possess the same direct rotational decomposition, although their hidden trajectories may still admit general spectral analysis across ordered positions. 10 11.5 Governance independence 

A governed execution layer should reduce inadmissible terminal transitions without requiring uniform suppression of internal trajectory diversity. This can be tested by comparing trajectory diversity, semantic continuity, and boundary violations under governed and ungoverned conditions. A successful result would show that authority control and representational diversity are independent dimensions. 

# 12 Limits of the Framework 

The framework does not establish that all semantic structure is harmonic, that Fourier modes are the optimal basis for hidden-state interpretation, or that phase coherence is a universal predictor of hallucination. It does not equate RoPE with classical cross-power spectral density, wave propagation, or quantum evolution, and it does not claim that attention is a linear time-invariant filter. Its defensible claims are more precise: 1. Ordered hidden-state sequences admit spectral decomposition. 2. RoPE implements position through explicit rotations in paired coordinate planes. 3. Rotary query–key scores decompose into magnitude-weighted cosine terms whose phases depend on content and relative position. 4. Phase alignment places mathematical bounds on the corresponding pre-softmax score. 5. Properly constructed paired modal coordinates permit phase-sensitive analysis of hidden-state trajectories. 6. Semantic continuity and institutional admissibility remain distinct, regardless of how coherent the model’s internal trajectory appears. These claims establish a research framework rather than a completed empirical theory. 

# 13 Future Research 

The framework becomes scientifically consequential only if its phase variables are identifiable, its predictions survive comparison with simpler geometric measures, and its governance claims remain independent of representational coherence. The next research program should therefore proceed through explicit tests rather than broader analogy. 

13.1 Basis identifiability and invariant phase structure 

Hidden-state phase is meaningful only relative to a reproducible paired basis. Future work should compare Fourier pairs, paired principal or singular directions, dynamic modes, and task-trained orthogonal probes under a common evaluation protocol. Each construction should be fitted on a training split, fixed before evaluation, and aligned across runs by a declared orientation convention. The analysis should report both coordinate-level phase and subspace-level invariants, separating effects that survive within-pair rotation from effects created by an arbitrary basis choice. A basis earns explanatory standing only if its phase variables predict held-out behavior beyond cosine similarity, subspace angle, and centered kernel alignment. 11 13.2 From pairwise score bounds to attention-row guarantees 

Lemma 1 controls one pre-softmax query and key score while holding pair magnitudes fixed. A stronger theory should incorporate the complete row of competing logits, the causal mask, perturbations in magnitude, and the margin between the selected key and its nearest competitor. This would permit lower bounds on normalized attention probability rather than compatibility alone. The subsequent problem is compositional: determine when local phase stability survives head aggregation, residual addition, normalization, and multilayer propagation. Such results would identify the precise conditions under which a local rotary guarantee has, or lacks, downstream behavioral force. 

13.3 Causal tests of continuity 

Correlation between modal coherence and successful task completion would not establish mechanism. Controlled interventions should rotate or attenuate selected modal coordinates while preserving relevant norms and non-target subspaces, then measure the effect on objective retention, contradic-tion, unsupported elaboration, repetition, and final-answer accuracy. These interventions should be compared with matched geometric perturbations and random subspace controls. The decisive question is whether phase-targeted interventions produce predictable, mode-specific changes that simpler activation-distance measures do not explain. 

13.4 Architecture and context-length generalization 

The rotary decomposition predicts explicit relative-phase structure in RoPE-based attention, but the broader hidden-trajectory analysis may extend to models using additive, bias-based, or learned positional schemes. Matched-model studies should distinguish what is specific to rotary coordinates from what follows merely from ordered context. Within RoPE systems, experiments should vary base frequencies, scaling rules, sequence length, and displacement range to determine where local phase alignment ceases to predict score stability. This would connect the theory to long-context behavior without presuming that extrapolation failures share a single spectral cause. 

13.5 Formal separation of continuity and admissibility 

Execution-boundary governance should be tested as an external predicate over candidate transitions, not as a semantic property inferred from hidden-state coherence. Experiments should compare governed and ungoverned trajectories while measuring boundary violations, false acceptance, false rejection, task completion, and internal trajectory diversity. The central hypothesis is that inadmis-sible transitions can be reduced without collapsing representational variety or requiring a uniform coherence threshold. Under this design, coherence remains a diagnostic variable; it never becomes evidence of authority. 

13.6 Falsification criteria 

The framework should be rejected or narrowed if reproducible paired bases cannot be identified, if phase measures fail to outperform geometric baselines on held-out tasks, if targeted phase interventions lack specific causal effects, or if the proposed governance separation yields no measurable reduction in inadmissible transitions independent of generic output suppression. These failure conditions are not peripheral. They define the boundary between a useful spectral theory and an attractive redescription of familiar geometry. 12 14 Conclusion 

Transformer language models are geometric computational systems, but their geometry contains ordered and rotational structure that admits a spectral description. Across context positions, hidden representations form sequences that can be decomposed into positional modes. Within RoPE, paired query and key coordinates possess explicit complex phases, and relative position enters their interaction as angular displacement. The resulting attention score is assembled from magnitude-weighted phase correlations before masked softmax converts those correlations into competitive influence. This structure supports a disciplined account of modal reinforcement, attenuation, and phase-sensitive continuity. It also clarifies the limits of coherence. A model may maintain strong internal alignment while drifting from an initiating objective. It may preserve semantic structure while losing evidential grounding. It may produce a coherent continuation that exceeds delegated authority. The passage from probability to consequence therefore cannot be governed by coherence alone. Spectral analysis may reveal whether a trajectory preserves relational structure. Governance determines whether that trajectory remains admissible. phase alignment preserves compatibility; semantic continuity preserves relation; governance preserves authority. A trustworthy architecture must understand all three—and must never confuse one for another.
